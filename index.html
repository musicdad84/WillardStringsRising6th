Music Is a Workout for Your Brain!

Learning a string instrument challenges your brain in exciting ways. When you play violin, viola, cello, or bass, you're reading music, listening carefully, moving your fingers, and keeping a steady beat—all at the same time. Playing in an orchestra adds another skill: working together to create something amazing!

🧠 Focus & Memory

Reading notes, remembering rhythms, and following a conductor give students regular practice with attention and memory.

🎻 Coordination

Using both hands in different ways while listening to your sound develops fine-motor coordination and timing.

🎵 Listening & Problem-Solving

Musicians learn to notice details, adjust their sound, and solve musical challenges as they practice.

🤝 Teamwork & Confidence

Orchestra teaches students to listen to one another, contribute to a group, work toward goals, and celebrate progress together.

Music learning engages many brain systems at once. Research suggests musical training can support certain listening, motor, and cognitive skills, although it does not guarantee higher grades or IQ. The best benefit? Making music with friends and friends with music.

Advanced Opportunities

Already play an instrument? Students with previous experience can receive extra challenges in sixth-grade strings and may have opportunities to perform with older students. As their skills grow, they can work toward advanced ensembles and auditions. These opportunities are generally for students who have been playing for several years.

🎻 Perform with 7th or 8th Grade

Experienced sixth graders may have the opportunity to perform with the seventh- or eighth-grade orchestras at concerts.

⭐ Chamber Strings

An advanced ensemble for students who have developed strong playing and ensemble skills.

🏅 All-County Orchestra

An opportunity for experienced musicians to play alongside students from other schools.

🌟 Junior Regional Orchestra

A challenging regional orchestra opportunity for advanced, experienced string players.

Try an Instrument Before You Choose!

At our instrument selection events, rising 6th graders get a mini-lesson on each instrument, with help from experienced student volunteers. Try all four and discover which feels right for you!

How it works: Students try all four instruments with guidance from middle and high school volunteers. Students stay seated in a music room while volunteers rotate through the rooms. Listen for the sound you like and notice how each instrument feels.

Before leaving: Parents complete the instrument selection form using the QR code provided at the event. Families can ask about rentals and supplies; Day Violins and Encore Strings are listed as participating rental shops in the event handout.

Sixth grade is the year to begin a new school band or strings instrument. Guitar is an option beginning in seventh grade.

Questions from Families

Here's what to know before your student begins orchestra. Read the Instrument Tryout Night handout ↗

Does my child need to know how to play?

No! Sixth grade is designed for beginners. Students with prior experience are also welcome and can receive extra challenges.

Does my child need an instrument at home?

Yes, students need access to an instrument for practice. We generally recommend renting rather than buying. Cellos are rented from a music shop and kept at home; basses are rented from the school for the year. School instruments are available for cello and bass students to use during the school day.

How are students graded?

Orchestra is a graded elective. Grades reflect effort, improvement, prepared participation, practice, and performances.

What supplies are needed?

We recommend renting from a local music store rather than buying. Please check the separate supply list for required tuner and shoulder-rest brands.

What does bass rental cost?

School bass rental is $100 for the year. The rented bass stays at home for practice, and students use a school instrument during class.

How much should students practice?

About 5–10 minutes a day. Consistency is more important than long sessions.

Will there be concerts?

Yes! Sixth graders perform in two concerts, one in winter and one in spring. Concerts take place in February and May. There is one after-school rehearsal before the May concert; dates and rehearsal details are shared early in the school year.

Is financial help available?

Yes. Families who need financial assistance should contact the orchestra directors.

Who will teach my child?

Mr. Anderson and Ms. Yoo teach the Willard orchestra program throughout middle school.
